# Human voice

Everything here applies to prose a human will read and judge: emails, replies,
posts, blogs, articles, bios, cover letters, profile copy. It does not apply to
code, commit messages or PR bodies.

The reference is
[Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).
The list below is the operative part of it.

## The test before sending

Read the draft aloud. If a sentence is one you would not say to the person's
face, cut it or rewrite it flat. That single check catches most of what follows.

## Banned vocabulary

Never use these words in prose. There is almost always a plainer word.

> delve · tapestry · testament · landscape · realm · navigate (figurative) ·
> underscore · showcase · leverage (as a verb) · boasts · vibrant · robust ·
> crucial · pivotal · vital · intricate · meticulous · seamless · groundbreaking ·
> renowned · profound · enduring · foster · garner · bolster · elevate ·
> interplay · harness · unlock · embark · myriad · plethora · nestled ·
> "in the heart of" · "diverse array" · "rich history"

## Banned constructions

**Negative parallelism.** "Not just X, but Y." "It's not X, it's Y."
"X rather than Y" used for effect. This is the single loudest AI tell.
Say the positive thing once.

**Trailing present participles.** Sentences ending "...highlighting the
importance of", "...underscoring the need for", "...reflecting a broader
shift", "...ensuring that", "...fostering". Stop at the fact.

**Rule of three.** Three adjectives, three examples, three clauses, when one or
two would do. Real writing has uneven lists.

**Copula avoidance.** "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "represents",
"marks" where "is" or "was" is meant. Also "features"/"offers"/"maintains"
where "has" is meant.

**Elegant variation.** Calling one thing three names across three sentences to
avoid repeating a word. Repeat the word. It reads as confidence.

**Vague attribution.** "Industry reports suggest", "experts argue",
"observers have noted", "studies show" with no study named.

**The formulaic close.** "Despite its challenges…", "Overall…",
"In conclusion…", "Looking ahead…". End on the last real point.

**Reader-address filler.** "Let's dive in", "you might notice", "it's worth
noting that", "needless to say".

## Formatting tells

- **Em dashes.** At most one per email. Prefer a comma, a full stop, or a
  rewrite. Overuse is the most recognisable visual tell.
- **Bold.** Not for atmosphere. Bold a number or a name if the reader must not
  miss it, otherwise none.
- **Straight quotes and apostrophes** in plain-text contexts (email, X, forms).
  Curly ones give away a paste from a rich-text generator.
- **No emoji as bullets or section markers.** One at the end of a friendly line
  is fine if that is how Jannet already writes.
- **No Title Case headings.** Sentence case.
- **Lists only for actual lists.** Not to break up prose that should be a
  paragraph.

## Voice

Short declaratives. Specifics over adjectives: "24s to 3.9s on a T4" beats
"significant performance gains". Say the number, name the tool, state what
happened. Contractions are fine. Starting a sentence with "But" or "So" is
fine.

State the weakness plainly when there is one. "Node/Express is where I'd be
ramping, not leading" earns more trust than any amount of positive framing, and
it survives the first technical conversation.

Do not explain the same point twice in different words. If a paragraph restates
the one above it, delete it.

## Length

An email to a founder is under 200 words. An X reply is one to three lines. A
LinkedIn post is under 150 words. If a draft runs long, the fix is deleting a
whole paragraph, not trimming adjectives from every sentence.

## Before returning a draft

1. Search it for the banned vocabulary above. Any hit gets rewritten.
2. Count em dashes. More than one in an email means rewrite.
3. Find every "not just… but" and every trailing participle. Remove them.
4. Read the first and last sentence together. If they say the same thing,
   cut the last one.
5. Check the length ceiling for the format.
