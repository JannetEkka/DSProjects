# Jannet's own ideas

Ideas Jannet had while learning AI/ML (shared 11-09). They're for any future hackathon or project where one fits; each still gets the web check in [lessons.md](../lessons.md) (lesson 0) and a fresh look at the hackathon's rules before it's proposed.

1. **rockab.ai (rockabye):** one collaboration space where artists and AI make rock music together. Its main job is turning any song into a rock genre of the user's choice, and it can turn a hummed melody into any instrument. Hum-to-song tools exist on their own (Hum2Song, AIMakeSong, AutoBandStudio, checked 11-09), but not as one space where artists collaborate across all of it (Jannet).
2. **Smart home, one set:** every appliance in the house connected and controlled from one phone app, sold as a complete home set rather than one smart appliance at a time. Samsung's SmartThings and the Matter standard cover parts of this; the pitch can be built for another company, or for Jannet's own home, and shown in a hackathon (Jannet).
3. **devlang:** a Python package for "anything automation": CI/CD, checking a web stack is reachable. Parked until it has one specific problem to start from.
4. **calmdown:** understands the person (including through astrology and other personal frames), asks what happened, and picks a sound frequency for them to meditate on, inspired by 432 Hz. Suggested to Jannet by a meditation expert. Evidence read on 11-10: small randomized trials found music lowered anxiety, and a few found a slightly larger heart-rate drop at 432 Hz than at 440 or 443 Hz (about 3–5 bpm), but results are mixed and the dental trial found 440 Hz worked as well. So any health claim must quote those trials as they are, and say the effect may come from the music itself. Apps with mood check-ins and "healing frequencies" exist (Yangu, Beeing, checked 11-10); the frequency-picking from what the person shares is the part to check again before proposing it.
